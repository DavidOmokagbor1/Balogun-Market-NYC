## The line

A curated luxury marketplace bridging African creativity with global fashion culture — starting in New York City.

Inspired by Lagos’s Balogun Market. Positioned alongside Dover Street Market, Browns, and The Folklore.

Fashion only. The roster is the product.

---

## Why this exists

African luxury is made and shown. It is still too hard to buy from the United States with the presentation and trust that luxury requires.

Generic platforms flatten the house. Archives treat garments as artifacts. Balogun **selects**, **tells**, and **sells**.

Mission: to elevate African luxury by giving designers a world-class platform to be discovered, celebrated, and purchased — starting in New York City.

Luxury shoppers looking for newness. Fashion Week attendees. Stylists, editors, curators. Diaspora clients who want cultural authority without costume.

## How it is built (for partners)

The public site is the house. Shopify is the till.

- Next.js storefront (Vercel)
- Shopify Storefront API + hosted checkout (`balogunmarketnyc.com`)
- No AI search. No generated provenance. No fake membership.

Four pillars: Cultural Authority · Luxury Craftsmanship · Global Access · Community & Commerce.

## Investor snapshot (paste-ready)

Balogun Market NYC is building the considered retail layer African luxury has been missing in the U.S.: a highly edited multi-brand house, not a marketplace dump and not an archive. V1 is a live editorial storefront plus Shopify commerce, opening with two Lagos houses chosen for cut and craft. The wedge is New York — shoppers, stylists, and Fashion Week — with pop-ups and further houses after the first drop converts. Comparables in posture, not in scale: Dover Street Market, Browns, The Folklore. Ask: capital and relationships that get the first collection on the floor and the first order through the till.
